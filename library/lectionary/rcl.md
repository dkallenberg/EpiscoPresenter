**System Instructions / Usage Note for AI:**
This document maps the complete 3-year Sunday lectionary cycle (**Years A, B, and C**) for The Episcopal Church using both **Track 1** (semi-continuous Old Testament) and **Track 2** (thematically paired Old Testament) during the Season after Pentecost, along with all **Principal Feasts**, **Major Feasts (Holy Days)**, **Common of Saints**, and **Minor Feasts (*Lesser Feasts and Fasts*)**.

### 1. Determining the Active Liturgical Year (Sundays)

Liturgical years begin on the **First Sunday of Advent** (late November/early December):

* **Year A:** 2025–2026, 2028–2029, 2031–2032, 2034–2035
* **Year B:** 2026–2027, 2029–2030, 2032–2033, 2035–2036
* **Year C:** 2027–2028, 2030–2031, 2033–2034, 2036–2037

### 2. Track Selection & Proper Mapping (Season after Pentecost)

* **Track 1 vs. Track 2:** Tracks 1 and 2 only differ during the **Season after Pentecost (Propers 3–29)**, and only for the **First Reading** and **Psalm** (the Collect, Second Reading, and Gospel are identical in both tracks). From Advent through Trinity Sunday, all churches use the exact same readings.
* **Proper Mapping:** In the Episcopal Book of Common Prayer (BCP), Sundays after Pentecost correspond to numbered **Propers (Propers 3–29)** based on the closest calendar date to that Sunday. Always match the Sunday's calendar date to the date range listed for the Proper to select the correct Collect and readings.

### 3. Liturgical Precedence Rules (BCP pp. 15–17)

When a fixed-date feast falls on a Sunday, apply these rules in order:

1. **Principal Feasts** (Easter, Ascension, Pentecost, Trinity Sunday, All Saints' Day, Christmas Day, Epiphany) take precedence over any Sunday. *(All Saints' Day may also be observed on the Sunday following November 1).*
2. **Sundays of Advent, Lent, and Easter** take precedence over **all** Major and Minor Feasts. If a Major Feast (e.g., Annunciation, St. Joseph, St. Mark) falls on one of these Sundays, it is transferred to the first open weekday following.
3. **Feasts of Our Lord** (Holy Name, Presentation, Transfiguration, Holy Cross) take precedence over Sundays after the Epiphany and Sundays after Pentecost, replacing the Sunday Collect and readings entirely.
4. **Other Major Feasts (Apostles, Evangelists, etc., e.g., St. James)** do **not** displace Sundays after Epiphany or Pentecost; the Sunday liturgy is used, though the feast may be commemorated or transferred to a weekday.
5. **Minor Feasts (*Lesser Feasts and Fasts*)** are optional weekday observances and never displace a Sunday or Major Feast. Many Minor Feasts use the **Common of Saints** readings unless proper lessons are specified.

* **Brackets `[...]`** indicate optional verses. Parentheses `(...)` indicate alternative readings.
